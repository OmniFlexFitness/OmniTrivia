# The categories

OmniTrivia ships with twenty category names. The first ten are the ones the
game generates for and draws an icon for on the wheel; the other ten are in
the pool a room votes on. Spell the name exactly as below in the `category`
column of every row; that name becomes the round's name on screen.

Any other category name also works (a venue's own name, "Florida", "Taylor
Swift"). It plays as its own round with a grey question-mark icon. Write the
note for it yourself in the same spirit: the famous parts of the subject at
the level asked, never the deep cuts.

Each note says what the category covers at these levels and where the line
is. Cover most of the subtopics in a 30-question set so it is spread across
the category rather than clustered on one corner of it.

## The ten built-in categories

**Science.** Everyday science: planets, the body, animals, weather, states of
matter, famous inventions, the periodic table's household elements, simple
forces. School-lab facts, not research.

**History.** The famous events and names everybody has heard of: world wars,
presidents, ancient Egypt and Rome, explorers, the Moon landing, the Titanic,
famous kings and queens. Centuries and decades, never exact years.

**Geography.** Capitals, countries, continents, oceans, famous landmarks and
rivers, mountains, flags of big countries, US states and their nicknames.

**Pop Culture.** The biggest films, TV shows, celebrities, memes and franchises
of the last 40 years. The things that were everywhere, not deep cuts.

**Sports.** The rules, equipment, famous teams, biggest events and most famous
athletes of the major sports. No seasons, scores or stat lines.

**Tech.** Everyday technology: phones, apps, websites, famous companies and
founders, what common things are called. No version numbers or specs.

**Art.** The most famous paintings, sculptures, artists and styles, the ones
on postcards and in every textbook, plus basic colour and craft terms.

**Literature.** The most famous books, authors and characters: school reading
lists, blockbusters, children's classics. Plots and characters, not
publication dates.

**Music.** The biggest artists, bands, songs, instruments and genres across
the decades. Hits, not B-sides.

**Food.** Common dishes, ingredients, cuisines, drinks and kitchen basics.
Things people cook, order and eat.

## The ten pool categories

**Fitness.** Common gym knowledge: major muscles, classic exercises, basic
training terms, famous athletes and events. Nothing that needs a
certification.

**Nutrition.** The basics people see on a label: macronutrients, vitamins,
common foods and what they are known for. No dosages or study findings.

**Anatomy.** The body as taught in school: major organs, bones, muscles and
senses, and what they do.

**Supplements.** The household names: protein, creatine, caffeine, vitamins,
electrolytes. What they are and what they are famous for. No research claims
and no health claims.

**Movies.** Famous films, actors, directors, quotes and characters. Box-office
hits and classics everybody has seen or heard of.

**Video Games.** The biggest franchises, characters, consoles and companies.
Mario, Pokemon, Minecraft, not patch notes.

**Space.** Planets, the Moon, the Sun, astronauts, famous missions and
everyday sky facts.

**Animals.** Well-known animals and what makes them famous: the biggest, the
fastest, where they live, what they eat, their babies and groups.

**Mythology.** The famous Greek, Roman and Norse gods, heroes and monsters,
and the stories everybody half-remembers.

**Internet Culture.** The sites, apps, memes and creators everybody has used
or seen. Mainstream, not niche communities.

## The OmniFlex categories, a note

Fitness, Nutrition, Anatomy and Supplements are OmniFlex Fitness's home turf,
and the temptation is to write them at the level Bertin knows them. Do not.
The room is the same bar crowd as for every other category. "Which muscle is
the biceps attached to?" is a `school` question; "What is the ATP-PC system's
approximate duration?" is not a trivia question at any level here. Keep
supplement questions to what a thing is and what it is famous for, and never
to what it does for health.
