# Prizes: splitting a pot across two games in one night

How to divide prizes when a night runs **two games** (two full runs of the
rounds, back to back, via **PLAY AGAIN**), built around the three places the
app calls at the end of every game: **Champion**, **Runner-up** and
**Redemption**.

This is a recommendation, not something the app enforces. The app tells you
*who* won each place; how much each place is worth is yours to set.

---

## 1. What the app gives you to award

The shape of a game, which the prizes follow: **qualifying rounds** nobody
goes out of (wins count ×1.5 on the total), a **bracket seeded on those
totals**, the **Redemption Table** for anybody knocked out, and a
**best-of-3 final**. Load enough rounds for the whole thing — the lobby says
how many the room needs (two qualifying rounds, eight players and a best-of-3
final is up to 7).

At the end of every game the projector, the host desk, the remote and every
phone show the same three names:

| Place | Who gets it | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Champion** | Last player standing in the bracket — or, if the rounds run out first, the top of the standings (unbeaten players ahead of the loser's bracket, then total score). | The main event. |
| **Runner-up** | The finalist who lost the best-of-3 series. If the rounds ran out, the next player in the same order the champion was picked in. | Rewards a deep run, and gives the semi-finalists something real to play for. |
| **Redemption** (third place) | Most **redemption points** — every point a player answered their way to, in every round of the game, before the win bonus — among players who are *not champion or runner-up*. | Rewards the best night of trivia outside the final, and keeps everybody who loses early playing hard: every point they score still counts. |

Two things about Redemption matter for prize design:

- **It counts the whole game, for everybody.** Redemption points build from
  round one — qualifying, the bracket and the Redemption Table alike — and
  everybody plays every round's questions, so it is a like-for-like count of
  who answered best all night. Going out early costs a player nothing here,
  which is the reason to stay; it is not a head start either. The ×1.5 win
  bonus is left out, because the bracket already pays for wins through
  Champion and Runner-up.
- **It is one prize per person per game.** A player who comes back as a
  wildcard and goes on to win the bracket takes Champion, not Redemption —
  the redemption prize passes to the next player down. The app already does
  this.

---

## 2. The recommendation

### 2.1 Split the night 40 / 60 between the two games

**Game 2 is worth more than game 1.** That is the single most important rule
for a two-game night.

- **Why:** the biggest risk of a two-game night is the room thinning out
  after game 1 — everybody who lost early in game 1 has a reason to leave. A
  bigger game 2 gives them a reason to stay, and it gives late arrivals a
  game worth joining.
- **Why not 50 / 50:** an even split makes game 2 optional. People who did
  badly in game 1 leave; the room gets smaller; game 2 is less fun for the
  people who stayed.
- **Why not steeper (30 / 70):** game 1 has to matter too, or nobody takes it
  seriously and it becomes a warm-up the room talks through.

### 2.2 Inside each game: 55 / 25 / 20

| Place | Share of that game's pot |
| --- | --- |
| Champion | **55%** |
| Runner-up | **25%** |
| Redemption | **20%** |

- **Redemption is a real prize, not a participation trophy.** At 20% it is
  close enough to runner-up that a player knocked out in round one still has
  something worth playing every remaining round for.
- **It is deliberately below runner-up.** Reaching the final takes a player
  out of the running for redemption. If redemption paid more than runner-up,
  a player already well clear on points would be better off losing the
  semi-final on purpose to stay eligible for it. Keeping it under runner-up
  makes throwing a match a bad trade — you give up a sure 25% and a shot at
  55% for a shot at 20%.
- **Champion keeps a clear majority.** The bracket is the competition; the
  champion should go home with the biggest prize of the game, every time.

### 2.3 Worked example: a $200 night

| | Game 1 (40% = $80) | Game 2 (60% = $120) | Night total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Champion (55%) | $45 | $65 | $110 |
| Runner-up (25%) | $20 | $30 | $50 |
| Redemption (20%) | $15 | $25 | $40 |
| **Total** | **$80** | **$120** | **$200** |

(Rounded to friendly numbers. For $100, halve it; for $300, multiply by 1.5.)

With bar tabs or gift cards instead of cash, keep the same *ratios*: game 2's
champion should get the best prize of the night, and redemption should always
be something people actually want.

## 3. Should one person be able to win both games?

**Recommendation: yes, at launch — and switch on a cap only if one regular
starts winning every week.**

- **Why allow it:** trivia is a game of skill. Capping the best player
  punishes the thing the night is about, and it invites the champion of game
  1 to coast through game 2, which is unfair to whoever they are drawn
  against.
- **When to cap:** if the same person wins *both* championships two or three
  weeks running, newer players will stop believing they can win, and
  attendance follows. Then switch on:

  > **Defending champion rule:** the game 1 champion can still win game 2
  > and gets the title (and a non-cash prize — merch, a bar tab), but game 2's
  > champion cash goes to the highest finisher who did not win game 1.

- Within a single game no cap is needed — the app already gives each player
  at most one of the three places.

## 4. Alternatives, and why they come second

| Structure | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **Winner takes all, each game** | Maximum stakes for the top two or three players, nothing for everybody else. Exactly the room-empties-early problem this update exists to fix. |
| **50 / 50 between games** | Simple, but gives no reason to stay for game 2 — see 2.1. |
| **Top three by total score** | Ignores the bracket, so the head-to-head format stops meaning anything. The bracket should decide the main prizes. |
| **One prize for the combined total across both games** | Rewards whoever played both games well, which sounds good — but the app resets scores on PLAY AGAIN, so the host has to add two sets of numbers by hand at the end of the night. Worth it only as a small extra (5–10% of the pot), and only if you note both games' final standings. |
| **A season ladder** (points for placing each week) | The strongest retention tool there is, but it lives outside the app (a spreadsheet, for now). A good next step once weekly attendance is steady. |

## 5. Making the incentives land in the room

A prize nobody knows about changes nobody's behaviour. Say it out loud:

- **Before game 1:** "Every point you score tonight, in every round, counts
  towards your redemption points — the most of them outside the final takes
  third place. Knocked out early? Don't leave. You keep playing every round on
  the Redemption Table, still building them, and the best round there can get
  you back into the bracket as a wildcard."
- **Before game 2:** "Game 2 is worth more than game 1." Say the numbers.
- **Every round:** the last question counts double, and the screens say so —
  the host can call it out too ("double points, anybody's match").
- **At the end:** read all three places off the podium screen. Make the
  redemption winner's moment as loud as the runner-up's.

The big screen already shows the Redemption Table and wildcard rules on the
wall between rounds, and every phone tells a knocked-out player what they are
still playing for.

## 6. A legal note (Florida)

Not legal advice — check with the venue, and with an attorney if real money
is involved.

- **Prizes for skill, funded by the house or a sponsor, with free entry** is
  the clean set-up, and it is what this guide assumes.
- **If players pay an entry fee and the fees fund the pot**, that starts to
  look like betting on a contest of skill, which Florida restricts (Fla. Stat.
  § 849.14). Keep entry free, or fund prizes separately from any fee.
- **Never award a prize by chance** (a raffle, a random draw among players)
  when entry costs anything. Drawings by chance are lotteries under Florida
  law (Fla. Stat. § 849.09) unless they fit the narrow exceptions for
  charities (§ 849.0935) or "no purchase necessary" promotions (§ 849.094).
  Every prize in this guide is decided by play, not by chance.

---

## Key takeaways

- **Split the night 40 / 60** — game 2 must be worth more, or the room thins
  out after game 1.
- **Inside each game, 55 / 25 / 20** for Champion / Runner-up / Redemption.
  Redemption is third place: the most points answered across every round, by
  anybody who is not champion or runner-up.
- **Redemption is real money, but always less than runner-up**, so losing on
  purpose never pays.
- **One place per person per game** is built into the app; across games,
  allow back-to-back wins until one regular dominates, then use the defending
  champion rule.
- **Announce it.** The Redemption Table, wildcards and the double final
  question only keep people playing if they know about them.
